Enforcement Guidance: Reasonable Accommodation and Undue Hardship Under the American Disabilities Act, The employer should be
receptive to any relevant information or requests it receives from a third party acting on the individual’s behalf because the reasonable
accommodation process presumes open communication in order to help the employer make an informed decision.

A request for reasonable accommodation is the first step in an informal, interactive process between the individual and the employer. In some
instances, before addressing the merits of the accommodation request, the employer needs to determine if the individual’s medical condition
meets the ADA definition of “disability,” a prerequisite for the individual to be entitled to a reasonable accommodation.

PRIVACY ACT STATEMENT

Authority
Health and Human Services (HHS) is authorized to collect the requested information under the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, 29 U.S.C. 701 and
the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), 42 U.S.C. 12101. Additional authorities listed in the applicable System of Records Notices (SORNs)
include: 42 U.S.C. 2000e-16, 12101 through 12117, and 12201 through 12213; 5 U.S.C. 1302, 3301, 3302; and Executive Order (E.O.) 13164,
Establishing Procedures to Facilitate the Provision of Reasonable Accommodation, 65 FR 46565 (July 26, 2000).

Purpose
HHS’s Reasonable Accommodation Program staff will use submitted information to track and monitor reasonable accommodation requests
submitted by HHS employees and job applicants or their representatives.

Routine Uses
While the information requested is intended to be used primarily for internal HHS purposes, in certain circumstances it may be necessary to
disclose information to: a contractor or agent engaged by HHS to support accommodation request handling or other relevant agency functions;
a requesting labor organization when relevant to personnel representation; the Department of Justice (DOJ) or a court as necessary and
relevant to litigation or administrative proceedings; an adjudicative body (e.g., the Merit System Protection Board), arbitrators, and hearing
examiners to the extent necessary to carry out their authorized duties; or, to a person authorized to act on your behalf. A complete list of the
routine uses can be found in the SORNs associated with this collection of information, Accommodation Records About HHS Civilian Employees,

information may prevent RAC from processing a reasonable accommodation request.
