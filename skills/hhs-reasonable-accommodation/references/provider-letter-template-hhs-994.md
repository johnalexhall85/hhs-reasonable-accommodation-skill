*Private — Do Not Submit Without Provider Review*

Please review each section against your own clinical findings, revise anything that does not match your independent clinical judgment, and sign on your practice letterhead. ### (d) Reason [EMPLOYEE] Requires Accommodation, **OR** the Particular Accommodation Requested
 
**Accommodation Requested**
 
[EMPLOYEE] is requesting full-time telework as her reasonable accommodation. Removing the office environment entirely, rather than modifying it, is the appropriate accommodation because [EMPLOYEE]'s reported triggers — fluorescent/bright lighting, ambient noise, and fragrance exposure — are inherent features of the shared office environment that cannot be reliably eliminated through modification, and because the safety concern described in Section (c) above (inability to safely drive during or after an episode) is specific to her presence at a worksite away from home. [Please confirm or revise this characterization based on your clinical judgment.]
 
**Alternative Accommodations**
 
[EMPLOYEE] was provided a hard-walled private office with a door and controllable lighting as an interim measure. This measure has not been sufficient: episode frequency has increased since her return to onsite work despite this accommodation being in place, indicating that the private office does not adequately control her relevant triggers (e.g., ambient noise and fragrance exposure that a private office with a door does not fully eliminate). A modified work schedule alone would not address the safety concern described above, because episode onset is reported as unpredictable and typically occurs mid-day, meaning a schedule change would not reliably prevent an episode from occurring while she is at the worksite. The private office and any schedule modification are less effective than full-time telework, individually and collectively, because neither addresses the full combination of environmental triggers and the driving-safety consequence described above. [Please confirm or revise this characterization based on your clinical judgment.]

### (e) How the Accommodation Will Assist [EMPLOYEE] in Performing Essential Functions or Enjoying Benefits and Privileges of Employment
 
**Nexus**
 
[EMPLOYEE]'s essential job functions consist of stakeholder communication, data analysis, technical and project work, and written and oral communications — none of which require onsite presence as a function of the job itself. Full-time telework addresses the functional limitations described above by removing [EMPLOYEE] from the environmental triggers that provoke episodes (fluorescent lighting, ambient noise, fragrance exposure) and by eliminating the safety risk of needing to drive to or from a worksite during or after an active episode. By removing these barriers, full-time telework enables [EMPLOYEE] to perform her essential functions consistently, without the unpredictable interruption and safety risk created by onsite attendance. [Please confirm or revise this nexus explanation based on your clinical judgment.]
 
**Safety Consideration**
 
When an episode begins while [EMPLOYEE] is at the office worksite, she is unable to safely operate a motor vehicle to return home until her symptoms have sufficiently resolved. This confines her to the triggering environment for the remainder of the episode and creates a safety risk both to her and to others on the road if she were to attempt to drive while symptomatic. [Please confirm or revise this safety consequence based on your clinical assessment.]
 
**Consequences if Accommodation Is Not Provided**
 
If full-time telework is not provided, I would expect [EMPLOYEE] to continue experiencing episodes at the current or increased frequency while working onsite, given that episode frequency has already increased since her return to the office despite the interim private-office accommodation. This creates an ongoing safety risk related to post-episode driving, and is likely to result in continued disruption to her ability to perform essential functions requiring sustained attention, real-time communication, and written output during and following active episodes. [Please confirm or revise this expected outcome based on your clinical judgment and documented course of treatment.]

**Note on this structure:** This letter is drafted in full using the employee's intake information so that the provider's task is limited to reviewing, confirming, and signing — or making targeted clinical revisions — rather than authoring narrative content from scratch. Sections (a) through (e) map directly onto HHS-994's own lettered items so the letter is self-evidently responsive to the form's structure on its face. If any section cannot be fully drafted because intake did not capture sufficient detail, that gap should be resolved with the employee/representative before this letter is finalized — it should not be left as an open drafting task for the provider.
