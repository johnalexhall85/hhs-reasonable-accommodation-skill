**HHS Reasonable Accommodation Assistant**

**What This Project Does**

This Project helps HHS employees and their representatives prepare thorough, well-organized documentation for federal reasonable accommodation (RA) requests — the kind of documentation that holds up through the interactive process, RAC review, and (if needed) an EEO complaint or appeal.

It's built specifically around HHS Reasonable Accommodation Policy 126-1 and the Rehabilitation Act/ADA framework, and it's designed to close a common gap: **official HHS forms ask for the legal minimum. This tool helps you provide more than the minimum, to ensure you are effectively accommodated.**

**Who It's For**

  - **Employees** requesting an accommodation on their own behalf

  - **Representatives** (family members, attorneys, or other authorized reps) supporting an employee's request

  - Either can use it solo, and a rep and employee can also work from a shared chat together — the tool will ask up front which setup applies and adjust accordingly

**What It Helps You Do**

**Intake, structured in four private phases:**

1.  Basic info about the position and functional limitations

2.  A private brainstorm translating symptoms into workplace-relevant functional language and possible accommodations, for the employee to review and confirm

3.  Case history — prior requests, agency conduct, EEO status, open deadlines

4.  Document drafting, once the above is confirmed

**Documents it can draft:**

  - A compliance tracker (accommodation history, policy violations, open 45-day EEO windows)

  - A functional limitations and nexus document for the interactive process meeting

  - A formal response to a RAC's information request

  - A **formal accommodation request letter** to accompany HHS-995

  - **Provider letters** — drafted in full using your intake information, structured to map directly onto HHS-994's own required items, so your provider mainly needs to review, adjust, and sign rather than write from scratch

  - Cover notes explaining strategic framing to your provider in plain language

  - Professional correspondence to the RAC or agency

**Built-in strategy:**

  - Always uses functional (not diagnostic) language in anything sent to the agency

  - Frames telework as a workplace modification, not a commute accommodation

  - Proactively addresses alternative accommodations the agency might propose — even before they're offered — using JAN (Job Accommodation Network) guidance

  - Tracks every potential 45-day EEO deadline

  - Flags legally questionable provisions in HHS's own policy where relevant

**What It Won't Do**

  - It's not a lawyer and doesn't give legal advice — it'll tell you when to loop in EEO counsel

  - It won't discuss active EEO complaint strategy inside RA documents

  - It treats every specific date, name, or case detail in its reference templates as an example only — it never invents or assumes real case facts

**Privacy Note**

**This is a U.S. Government information system.** The login banner states that HHS routinely monitors, intercepts, and may inspect or seize any data on this system — nothing entered here is private from the agency. There's a narrow exception for privileged communications tied to attorney, psychotherapist, or clergy representation, but it typically doesn't apply to employees describing their own condition or non-attorney representatives assisting them. **Treat anything you type here as visible to HHS.** This governs your data regardless of any separate privacy commitment Anthropic makes as a vendor.

A few practical implications:

  - **Use placeholders** for names, case numbers, and identifiers where the templates allow it; substitute real information only when a document is ready to submit.

  - **Medical detail from intake is kept out of agency-facing documents**, but that's not the same as being invisible to HHS while it sits in this system.

  - **This tool is not HHS's official system of records.** Finalize and submit documents through the RAC's official channels — don't treat this Project as the case file itself.

  - **Shared chats** are flagged up front, with check-ins before anything is treated as final — but that only manages who's typing, not the monitoring described above.

**Getting Started**

Just start a new chat in this Project. You'll be asked a couple of quick setup questions (your role, and whether the chat is shared), then walked through the four-phase process at your own pace.
